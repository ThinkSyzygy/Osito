# Risk Management

## Purpose

Make uncertain events and their possible consequences visible, owned, mitigated, monitored, and explicitly accepted or closed.

## Inputs

- Risk statement using cause, uncertain event, and consequence
- Applicable configuration and affected objectives
- Evidence, assumptions, likelihood, impact, and detectability where used
- Owner, triggers, mitigations, contingency, and review date
- Related requirements, decisions, changes, tests, and actions

## Outputs

- Current risk register
- Prioritized mitigation and validation actions
- Residual-risk assessment
- Accepted-risk or closure record
- Escalation and contingency triggers

## Sequence

1. Identify the risk and distinguish it from an issue that has already occurred.
2. Search for an existing equivalent risk before creating another.
3. Define cause, event, consequence, scope, and evidence.
4. Assess exposure using the team's documented qualitative or quantitative model.
5. Select avoidance, reduction, transfer, monitoring, or acceptance.
6. Assign mitigation actions, contingency triggers, owner, and next review.
7. Gather evidence and update the current assessment in place.
8. Review residual risk and obtain acceptance where needed.
9. Close only when the exposure no longer applies or evidence shows the closure criterion is met.

## State and status model

Suggested states are `identified`, `analyzing`, `mitigating`, `monitoring`, `accepted`, `realized`, `closed`, and `superseded`. Severity and likelihood are attributes, not statuses. An accepted risk remains current until its expiry or review condition.

## Provenance and traceability

Retain the source, assessment basis, model version, changes in exposure, mitigation evidence, decision authority, and related records. Do not erase prior assessments; capture meaningful changes in an event history or version trail.

## Review and approval gates

- High-consequence or safety-related risks receive the configured specialist review.
- Risk acceptance requires a named authority, rationale, residual exposure, scope, and review or expiry condition.
- Closing or lowering a risk requires evidence, not optimism or schedule pressure.
- A realized risk creates or links to an issue, incident, or change record.

## Adaptation

Teams may use simple categories or quantitative models. Define scales and escalation rules centrally, avoid false precision, and never import generic scoring thresholds without validating them for the organization.

## Failure and uncertainty handling

Insufficient evidence is recorded as uncertainty and may itself trigger validation work. Disagreement over exposure remains visible with both rationales. Missing ownership or an overdue high-consequence review is escalated rather than silently tolerated.
