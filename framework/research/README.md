# Research

## Purpose

Answer bounded engineering questions with traceable sources, explicit uncertainty, lawful reuse, and a clear distinction between reported fact, analysis, and recommendation.

## Inputs

- Research question, decision context, scope, and exclusions
- Required freshness and source-quality expectations
- Permitted local and external sources
- Confidentiality, copyright, licensing, and citation constraints
- Known hypotheses, terms, and conflicting claims

## Outputs

- Research brief
- Source and claim index
- Findings separated into facts, inferences, assumptions, and open questions
- Conflicts, limitations, and recommended next steps
- Optional reviewed promotion into reusable knowledge

## Sequence

1. Define the question, audience, decision, boundary, and stopping condition.
2. List known facts, assumptions, terminology, and likely source classes.
3. Gather only permitted sources and record retrieval details.
4. Evaluate authority, relevance, freshness, independence, and reuse rights.
5. Extract claim-level evidence without excessive quotation.
6. Compare sources and preserve material disagreement.
7. Synthesize findings; label inference and confidence.
8. Identify gaps and whether testing, supplier input, or specialist review is needed.
9. Review citations, confidentiality, licensing, and decision relevance.
10. Publish the brief or promote a generalized knowledge article after approval.

## State and status model

Suggested states are `planned`, `collecting`, `synthesizing`, `in_review`, `accepted`, `published`, `superseded`, and `abandoned`. Individual claims may be `supported`, `contested`, `inferred`, `unverified`, or `outdated`.

## Provenance and traceability

Record title, author or issuing body, publication or revision date, retrieval date, locator, applicable configuration, and claim linkage. Distinguish primary sources from commentary. Preserve citations even when conclusions change.

## Review and approval gates

- Research scope and source permissions are confirmed before collection.
- High-stakes conclusions require qualified review and, where appropriate, testing.
- Promotion to reusable knowledge requires confidentiality, ownership, and citation review.
- Recommendations that mutate project state go through the relevant decision or change workflow.

## Adaptation

Configure preferred source classes by discipline. For fast-changing topics, add expiry or recheck dates. When licensed standards apply, cite the authorized source and record project interpretation without reproducing restricted text.

## Failure and uncertainty handling

If sources cannot resolve the question, report the evidence boundary and a next experiment or inquiry. Do not average incompatible claims. If a source cannot be lawfully retained or quoted, store only an allowed citation and original summary.
